# SCIENTIFIC_BOUNDARIES

**Status:** complete for v0

This document exists to prevent a specific failure: a reader seeing a spike raster of
127 400 neurons and concluding something about a fly. It separates four things that
are easy to conflate, and states what may and may not be concluded at each step.

---

## 1. Four separate things

```
CONNECTOME          structural wiring. Measured by electron microscopy,
                    proofread by humans, published by the FlyWire consortium.
                    This is biological data.

      ↓  (MelanoGraph adds dynamics that the connectome does not contain)

LIF MODEL           chosen computational dynamics. Two variables per neuron,
                    one uniform weight per synapse, no plasticity, no
                    neuromodulation. This is a MODEL, not data.

      ↓  (MelanoGraph runs it)

SIMULATED ACTIVITY  the result of the model. It is a property of the model,
                    not of the fly. It is reproducible, inspectable, and
                    entirely artificial.

      ‖  (NOT an arrow. These are different objects.)

BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY measurements from a real animal: electrophysiology,
                    calcium imaging, behavioural readouts.
```

The vertical double bar is the important part. **Simulated activity does not
"approximate" biological activity in any quantitative sense that MelanoGraph
establishes.** It is generated by a model whose excitability is set by a parameter the
upstream authors labelled a free parameter (`w_syn`). Nothing here is validated
against a recording.

---

## 2. What the connectome contributes, and what it does not

**Contributes** (FlyWire-derived, per `DATA_PROVENANCE.md`):

* which neurons exist (127 400, v630);
* which neuron connects to which, in which direction;
* how many synapses between each ordered pair;
* whether each connection is excitatory or inhibitory.

**Does not contribute**: everything about *dynamics*. The connectome has no time
constant, no threshold, no delay, no synaptic weight in mV, no reversal potential,
and no firing rates. Every one of those comes from the model.

A useful consequence: the connectivity statistics in `DATA_PROVENANCE.md` §3 are
statements about the fly's wiring and are as trustworthy as the published connectome.
The *activity* numbers are statements about the model and are trustworthy only as a
description of the model.

---

## 3. Calibration honesty

The single most important caveat, repeated here because it is easy to lose:

* `w_syn = 0.275 mV` is labelled `# Free parameter` in the upstream source. It is
  not derived from a measurement.
* `f_poi = 250` is justified upstream only by "250 is sufficient to cause spiking".
* The median connection in the data carries 3.6 synapses ≈ 1 mV of conductance, which
  gives a peak membrane excursion of ≈0.16 mV against a 7 mV threshold gap. A single
  presynaptic spike needs ≈162 synapses onto the same target to cross threshold alone.

So the model's absolute firing rates are set by a free parameter, and whether a given
neuron responds at all depends on convergent input reaching that threshold. MelanoGraph
does not tune these numbers to match anything, and does not claim its rates are
correct. It reports every rate with the parameters that produced it
(`summary.json → model_params`).

## 4. What MelanoGraph v0 may claim

With evidence recorded in `outputs/`:

1. The FlyWire-derived connectivity loads, is validated, and is represented without
   distortion — neuron IDs preserved, edge list exact, sign exact, no densification.
   *Evidence: `outputs/dataset_report.json`, `tests/test_data.py`.*
2. A leaky integrate-and-fire network with the published constants executes over that
   connectivity, with the published sub-step semantics. *Evidence: the exact
   integrator's fixed-point and closed-form tests in `tests/test_model_lif.py`.*
3. Controlled stimulation of a named sensory population produces activity that
   propagates beyond the stimulated neurons. *Evidence:
   `outputs/experiments/sugar_stimulation/sugar_stimulation.json`.*
4. Silencing a population changes the activity of other populations, and the changed
   populations are monosynaptically reachable from the silenced one. *Evidence:
   `outputs/experiments/silencing_test/silencing_test.json`.*
5. With no stimulus the model is exactly quiescent, so observed activity is
   attributable to the declared stimulus rather than to noise. *Evidence:
   `outputs/experiments/baseline_activity/baseline_activity.json`.*
6. Runs are reproducible: identical configuration and seed give an identical spike
   digest. *Evidence: `tests/test_cli.py::test_two_identical_runs_produce_the_same_spike_digest`.*

## 5. What MelanoGraph v0 may **not** claim

1. **That it reproduces the fly's brain activity.** The model is a simplification
   with a free excitability parameter. No comparison to a recording was performed.
2. **That a simulated response is a biological response.** A population lighting up
   in the simulation means the model's dynamics, applied to the modelled wiring,
   produced spikes there. It is not evidence that the animal's neurons do the same.
3. **That connectivity implies function.** A monosynaptic path from A to B in the
   model does not establish that A drives B *in vivo*; that requires a causal
   experiment in an animal. MelanoGraph's silencing experiment is causal *within the
   model* and nothing more.
4. **That the numbers match the published model.** They are not expected to
   (`MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md` §7): different RNG, different sub-step ordering in one
   upstream port, and 1 trial here versus 30 upstream. Direction and sparsity are
   comparable; values are not.
5. **Consciousness, agency, intent, decision-making, or any mental state.** The model
   has two state variables per neuron and no learning. There is nothing to be
   conscious.
6. **That the model's "brain" is the fly's whole nervous system.** It is the
   proofread central brain at completeness threshold 630. No ventral nerve cord, no
   peripheral ganglia, no body.

## 6. Rules MelanoGraph follows to keep the separation visible

These are enforced in code and tests, not only in prose:

* **Every plot title contains the literal string `simulated activity - not a
  biological measurement`** (`flybrain/analysis/plots.py::SIMULATED_TAG`). It is a
  module constant so it cannot be dropped from one figure and kept in another.
* **A subset is never called whole-brain.** `requested_mode` and `effective_mode` are
  separate fields; `mode_changed=True` and a note beginning "WHOLE-BRAIN RUN WAS
  REQUESTED AND WAS NOT DELIVERED." are written into `summary.json`, the plan notes,
  the banner, and the console. `tests/test_cli.py` asserts it.
* **A refusal is a refusal.** An infeasible whole-brain run exits with code 3 and
  writes no artefacts unless `--allow-fallback` was explicitly passed.
* **Truncation is reported.** `spike_recording_truncated` and
  `populations_with_missing_members` appear in every relevant summary.
* **Missing data is reported as missing.** `neuron_type: unavailable` with a reason,
  rather than an empty field; `unknown_sign` as its own count rather than folded into
  excitatory.
* **Model parameters travel with results.** `summary.json` embeds `model_params`,
  `stimuli`, `silenced_flywire_ids` and `backend`, so no number is ever orphaned from
  the settings that produced it.
* **The parameter table is generated from the code.** `tests/test_docs.py` fails if
  `docs/MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md` and `flybrain/model/lif.py` disagree.

## 7. What would be needed to make a stronger claim

Listed because it defines the distance between this and a scientific result, and
because the objective asks for known limitations to be recorded:

1. A quantitative comparison against the authors' published output under matched
   conditions (same RNG discipline or many trials, matched trial count, matched
   stimulation) — MelanoGraph currently compares only qualitatively and says so.
2. A comparison against *biological* measurements, which is a different and much
   harder task than against another simulation.
3. Sensitivity analysis over `w_syn` and `f_poi`, which would show how much of any
   observed pattern is the free parameter rather than the wiring.
4. Reachability analysis beyond one synapse: MelanoGraph resolves monosynaptic paths,
   which is a lower bound on influence and not a claim of functional drive.
5. Characterisation of the `Excitatory` annotation's accuracy, which is currently
   inherited unexamined.
